# gogh-xr

A Meta Quest 3 WebXR experience built with three.js, Vite and bun. Requirements live in [PRD.md](PRD.md) and the phase plans in [plans/](plans/).

## Make targets

`install build test lint fmt typecheck checkall checkall-ci bundle-check asset-check bake e2e dev dev-start dev-stop dev-restart preview pre-commit`

## Ports

- 4252: Vite dev server (`make dev`)
- 4253: Vite preview for the e2e suite (`make preview`)

## Gate rules

- `make checkall` is the fast gate while iterating (lint, typecheck, test, build, bundle-check).
- `make checkall-ci` must pass before any push. It adds gitleaks, `bun audit` and `asset-check`.
- `make e2e` is local-only and never runs in CI.
- `make bake` regenerates the Starry Night bake outputs (palette, flow field, region ids, and `lights.webp`, the full-resolution scan around the moon and stars) from the 3840 px scan and rewrites their PROVENANCE.md rows. It is not part of `checkall`, and `asset-check` verifies the committed outputs.
- Never pipe a gate. Redirect to a file, capture the exit code, then tail the log.
- No push without Paul's approval.

## Deploy

- A push to `main` deploys GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`) to `https://gogh-xr.pardev.net`.
- A Pages deployment is keyed by the commit SHA. Once a deploy for a SHA is cancelled or times out, every later attempt for that same SHA ends at once with "Deployment cancelled". This includes "re-run failed jobs", which also trips on the duplicate `github-pages` artifact, and `workflow_dispatch`. Push a new commit to deploy again.

## URL flags

Parsed in `src/diag/flags.ts`.

- `?diag`: installs `window.__goghDiag()` (renderer info, XR state, frame rates, camera pose, counts). `camera` is the world pose of the camera that rendered the last frame: three's XR camera while presenting, whose matrices include the rig, and the desktop camera otherwise.
- `?iwer`: installs the IWER Meta Quest 3 emulator. Only honoured in `--mode e2e` builds, never in production.
- `?bench=N`: runs the stroke benchmark with N strokes (default 100000, capped at 2,000,000). A non-numeric value uses the default. Strokes fill a full sphere, so every view direction reads the same. In XR, the right controller's A button steps fixed foveation through 0 (the app default), 0.5 and 1. The governor never runs in the bench, so the bench's own foveation steps are the only ones there. Each settings combination, foveation included, records fps, mean, p95 and p99 once its window settles. Results persist in localStorage under `gogh-xr.bench.results.v1`, show on the HUD, and appear as `benchResults` in `__goghDiag()`. Locomotion never runs in the bench, so on the desktop it is mouse look only, from the origin, with no WASD.
- `&mode=a2c|blend`: benchmark transparency mode, alpha-to-coverage (default) or blended. In the headset, the left controller's X button toggles the mode and resets the order to that mode's default.
- `&size=px`: benchmark stroke size in pixels (default 24).
- `&order=sorted|random`: benchmark draw order. Default is random for a2c and sorted for blend.
- `?hz=72|90|120`: requested XR target frame rate (default 90). Quest 3 reports `supportedFrameRates` of 72, 80, 90 and 120. In the headset, the left controller's Y button steps the target through the supported rates.
- `?beauty`: boolean, max-beauty mode. Targets 72 Hz with the max-beauty budget (500k drawn), and the governor starts from the max-beauty densities (`BEAUTY_BAND_DENSITY`). It wins over `?hz`, which it ignores. The bench (`?bench`) ignores it.
- `?slow=<ms>`: testing only. Busy-waits on the CPU for that many ms every frame, to force overruns for the governor. Clamped to 0-50. A malformed value means 0 (off). It is honoured in production builds on purpose: it is URL-only, it only slows the page that asks for it, and the P1.8 headset check needs it on the deployed build.
- `?zdiag=<mode>`: testing only, honoured in production like `?slow`. One stroke depth, order or filtering diagnostic (`src/render/zdiag.ts`): `nowrite`, `strokeorder` (the live depthFunc ALWAYS A/B, which leaves the world strokes under the base meshes), `paintorder`, `nosort`, `noa2c`, `sharp` (alpha-to-coverage kept, with twice the R3 coverage sharpening gain, so a half-pixel rim), `sharp4` (four times the gain, a quarter-pixel rim), `nomip`, `mip` or `freeze` (XR only). With `?diag`, `__goghDiag().zdiag` is `{ mode, applied, frozen }`. `bun scripts/shimmer-probe.ts <url>` measures per-mode flicker under seeded jitter (docs/verification.md, headset shimmer diagnosis).
- `?nogov`: boolean, testing only. Keeps the quality governor off, so densities stay fixed. The e2e specs that check stroke counts in XR use it, because headless Chromium's 60 Hz vsync already overruns the 90 Hz budget. Like `?slow`, it is honoured in production builds on purpose, since it is URL-only and affects only the page that sets it.
- `?painting=<id>`: boots straight into a painting's world from the registry (`src/paintings/registry.ts`), parsed like `?demo` (trimmed, lower-cased). Only `starry-night` is live: it builds the Starry Night world (`src/paintings/starry-night/index.ts`), binds its stroke batch to the governor, drops the bare scene's placeholder ground and grid, and stands the locomotion rig on the module's `spawnPose()`, which for Starry Night is the painter's spot (0, 0, 0) facing -Z, so the desktop eye is the painter's eye (0, 1.6, 0). `?painter` replaces that start with the painter camera. The rig carries the spawn in XR too, so a non-origin spawn needs no offset reference space. The `WorldHandle` that `build()` returns carries the batch, the built world's `ground` (`heightAt`, `normalAt`), `boundaryFactor(position)` and `blocked(x, z)` for P3 locomotion. Starry Night's `blocked` is true on a house's walls or the church footprint, grown by 0.3 m (`src/paintings/starry-night/footprints.ts`). An unknown or coming-soon id logs a warning and boots the bare scene. The bench ignores it. With `?diag`, `__goghDiag().world` holds `{ painting, seed, debug, counts, lightRankTies, total, times: { decodeMs, generateMs, scatterMs, batchMs, buildMs }, cache, strokes }`, where `counts` is per stroke layer plus `haze`. In `counts`, `sky` is the dome. `star` and `moon` are the static moon and star discs of `src/world/sky-lights.ts`, which are generated on every build outside the scatter cache, appended after the scatter and before the haze, and drawn in the backdrop's front tier over the dome. They are brushed like the dome around them (its stroke size, brushes and flow direction). The stars split finer only at colour edges such as their cores. The moon keeps the dome's stroke size, with finer strokes drawn on top only along the crescent's edges. Both thin out over a rim band to 1.5 anchor radii. Their colours come from `lights.webp`, the bake's full-resolution scan around each anchor, and from frame.webp past it. The batch pins them: each chunk orders them first and draws all of them at every band density, so governor shedding thins only the dome around them. `lightRankTies` counts the light strokes that share a front-tier rank with an overlapping one, judged in metres on the dome (0 at seeds 1 and 2, and a unit test finds no same-rank overlapping pair from the stored positions and sizes). Every haze stroke carries `SKY_LIGHT_CULL_FLAG`, and the stroke shader judges it per view, from that view's eye, against each disc's cone (`src/render/strokes/sky-light-cull.ts`). It collapses a stroke that could reach over a disc, and past the cone it shrinks the drawn size so each quad's area, and approximately the haze's coverage, ramps from 0 to full over a feather of `SKY_LIGHT_FEATHER` (3) anchor radii plus one haze half diagonal, so the haze has no hard rim in front of the moon or a star. The draw calls and instance counts are unchanged. `cache` is `hit`, `miss` or `bypass`, and `strokes` is the batch stats. No `?seed` means seed 1.
- `?bare`: boolean, honoured only together with `?diag` and a world. Every base mesh and the clear colour turn flat magenta `#ff00ff`, and the stroke batch is hidden. The coverage sweep uses it to calibrate: the view reads 100% magenta.
- `?coverage`: boolean, honoured only together with `?diag` and a world. Like `?bare`, but the strokes still render, so every magenta pixel is one the strokes leave bare. `tests/e2e/world-coverage.spec.ts` sweeps 24 poses with it and fails a pose over 0.5% bare. `?bare` wins when both are set.
- `?painter`: boolean, honoured only together with `?diag` and a world. Once the world module loads, and before the world builds, the desktop camera takes the world's `painterCamera()` (`src/paintings/world-module.ts`). Starry Night's comes from `src/paintings/starry-night/canvas-camera.ts`: eye (0, 1.6, 0) facing -Z, pitched up by `CANVAS_PITCH`, vertical FOV 60° and aspect `CANVAS_ASPECT`, which it keeps on a resize. The frame is then exactly canvas uv [0, 1]², so capture at the canvas aspect (1280 × 1014) to avoid a stretch. It is placed like `__goghPose`, so ground follow leaves it alone until WASD moves it. The P2.8 fidelity metric (`scripts/fidelity.ts`, `tests/e2e/fidelity.spec.ts`) captures through it.
- `?diag` also installs `window.__goghPose(x, y, z, yaw, pitch)`, which puts the desktop eye exactly at that position and heading through the rig (rig at y − 1.6, rig yaw, camera pitch). Yaw 0 faces -Z, and pitch ±π/2 looks straight up or down. Ground follow is suspended for a placed pose until the next movement input, then eases the rig back onto the ground, because the coverage poses carry absolute eye heights.
- `?seated`: boolean, seated mode (`src/locomotion/`). In XR the rig rises by the standing height (1.6 m) minus the seated head height, captured from the first frame whose camera holds a real XR head pose, once per session. On the desktop it is a no-op apart from `__goghDiag().rig.seated`.
- `?turn=snap|smooth`: the turn mode (`LocomotionSettings.turnMode`), trimmed and lower-cased. Snap is the default, and any other value keeps it. It exists so the e2e can exercise smooth turn until the P5 settings panel can set it.
- `?nocache`: boolean. Skips the IndexedDB scatter cache (`src/render/strokes/scatter-cache.ts`), so the world re-scatters its surface strokes on every load. It wins over `?cache`. The cache is also bypassed in `--mode e2e` builds, whenever `?seed` is given, whenever the build SHA is the `dev` fallback, and in every non-production build (the dev server) unless `?cache` is set. Only a production build without any of these uses the cache. The key is the build's short commit SHA (`__BUILD_SHA__`, defined in vite.config.ts) plus a hash of the scatter inputs: the seed, the fully resolved per-layer density and size tables, the per-mesh overrides, the generator seeds, the normals mode and `SCATTER_VERSION`. A new commit or a retuned table misses. Generator code changes miss only through the SHA, and uncommitted edits keep the same SHA, so bump `SCATTER_VERSION` when the scatter's output changes for the same inputs.
- `?cache`: boolean. Opts back into the scatter cache in a non-production build, so the cache path can be tested on the dev server. Vite evaluates the SHA once when the dev server starts, so it goes stale across commits there. `?nocache`, `?seed`, `--mode e2e` and the `dev` SHA still bypass the cache. A production build ignores the flag, because it uses the cache anyway.
- The quality governor (`src/render/governor.ts`) runs in XR for every scene with a stroke batch (`?strokesTest`, `?demo=canvas`, `?painting=starry-night`), never for `?bench`. The boot modes are exclusive: `?painting` with a live id wins over `?strokesTest` and `?demo`, and warns once that it ignored them. Its budget follows the frame rate the session reports, and the 72 Hz fallback commits only once `updateTargetFrameRate` resolves. It sheds foveation and fb scale first: on the headset (2026-11-06, `docs/verification.md`) foveation 0.5 measured -16%, while density bought nothing. The frame is GPU-bound, not CPU or draw-call bound, and pixel count alone does not explain it: 0.49x the pixels plus foveation 1 reads 18.71 ms against 17.2-18.1 ms for foveation 1 alone, and the GPU profiler puts about 54% of shading time on vertices (85M polygons/s of about 25 px). The order is foveation 0 to 0.5 (step `foveation`), fb scale 1 to 0.85, foveation to 1 (step `foveation-max`), then far, mid and near density. The foveation steps are not yet verified on a headset. Foveation goes through `renderer.xr.setFoveation`, which sets the projection layer's `fixedFoveation` live, and every judged frame re-asserts it if three reports another value. Density goes last: each density step grows the band's stroke size to keep its coverage, so it keeps the painted area, and on the headset it bought nothing measurable. A shed whose next window still overruns and is not at least 3% faster is undone at once (a `no-gain` event), and that step is held for 30 s, or until the reported frame rate changes or the session is interrupted. Each further hold of the same step, from no-gain or from recovery, doubles: 30, 60, 120, 240, then 300 s. A frame-rate change or a new session restarts the doubling, and a shed that pays restarts it for that step. The two foveation steps hold apart, so a 0.5 that reads as no-gain does not block 1. The density bands share one hold, `density`: under the size law one band's no-gain predicts the others', so a density no-gain holds all three. A held step counts as nothing left to shed, so the 72 Hz request still follows 3 s of overrun. A density step that would cut under 3% of the drawn strokes is skipped. Once the frame has overrun for 5 s with nothing left to shed and no 72 Hz request left to make (the session already runs at 72 Hz, or the request was applied or failed), it restores the top shed step (a `recover` event). If the next 2 s of windows overrun more than 3% worse than that overrun stretch, it sheds the step again (`recover-back`) and holds it from recovery (30 s, doubling). Otherwise it keeps the restore and holds the step from shedding (30 s, doubling). A frame no step helps therefore regains most of its shed steps (in simulation it settles within about three steps of full quality, up to 2.9 at worst over 120 s), and one that needs its sheds keeps its shed level. With `?diag`, `__goghDiag().governor` shows its mode, target, reported frame rate, rate request state, level, densities, framebuffer scale, foveation (0, 0.5 or 1), last window mean, notice, `judging`, `noGainHeld` (holds against shedding), `recoverHeld` (holds against recovery), where a hold is a step name or `density` for all three bands and a bounded history of shed, restore, shed-back, no-gain, recover, recover-back and rate events. `judging` is true only while a visible XR session presents. Desktop frames are never judged. `fbScale` is the requested scale. `fbScaleApplied` is the scale the device applied, measured from the returned viewport widths, and null until measured. IWER ignores the request, so it reads 1 there. Quest Browser ignores it too on the projection-layer path three 0.186 takes there (`fbScalePath` `projection-layer`, `fbScaleApplied` 1 at a requested 0.85), so on the headset the fb-scale step buys nothing and the no-gain rule holds it.
- `?hud`: boolean. Starts the perf HUD (`src/ui/perf-hud.ts`) visible. Without the flag the HUD starts hidden, and it can still be toggled on at any time. The governor's 72 Hz notice also shows it, hidden or not, because the DOM notice is invisible in immersive mode. The user can hide it again afterwards.
  - Toggle: the left controller's X button in XR, or the H key on desktop. Each press toggles once, and a held key's auto-repeat is ignored.
  - The HUD is built on its first show (`src/ui/hud-toggle.ts`). Until then nothing loads: no troika, no font fetch, no panel, and no `frame` diag key. Hiding keeps the HUD built but stops its per-frame work and the 4 Hz recompute.
  - The panel is troika text about 0.3 m wide, parented to the camera 0.6 m ahead and below the eye line, so it follows the head in XR and the viewer on desktop.
  - It shows fps, mean and p99 frame ms, GPU ms (or n/a), the target Hz, and the visible strokes followed by the visible chunks (`visible 183,412 strokes   112 chunks`, with `--` for both before a stroke batch is bound). When the governor runs, it also shows the level ("gov idle" in its place while the governor is not judging, as on desktop), the per-band densities, foveation, fb scale and the governor's notice. Otherwise it shows "governor off". When the applied fb scale is known and differs from the requested one by more than 0.01, it follows the fb scale as `(applied 1.00)`.
  - The text recomputes at 4 Hz while shown and is re-typeset only when it changes. The font is the local `public/fonts/NotoSans-Regular.ttf`.
  - With `?diag`, `__goghDiag().hud` is always registered outside `?bench`. Before the first show it is `{ visible: false, built: false }`. Once built it is `{ visible, built: true, text, updatedAt, typeset }`, where `visible` is the panel's own visibility.
  - Once built, `__goghDiag().frame` holds the live frame stats `{ t, fps, meanMs, p99Ms }`. These record only while the HUD is shown, and restart on each show.
  - `__goghDiag().xr.targetFrameRate` is the requested rate, which the HUD shows as the target when no governor runs.
  - With `?diag`, `__goghDiag().draw` is always registered: `{ calls, visibleChunks }`. `calls` is `renderer.info.render.calls` read right after each animation frame's render, so it is the last frame's draw calls. It counts every draw in that frame, including the base meshes, the HUD panel and the teleport arc, and in XR it covers both eyes. `visibleChunks` is the bound stroke batch's chunks that passed the last cull, or null with no batch bound. Each visible chunk is at most one draw call per view, because a chunk whose density rounds its instance count down to 0 issues no draw. The same count is `strokes.visibleChunks` in the `world`, `strokes` and `canvas` keys.
  - `?bench` ignores the flag and the toggle, because the bench keeps its own HUD and uses X for its mode. No HUD is ever built there.
  - The visibility choice is not persisted yet. That waits for the P5 settings panel.
- `?noanim`: boolean. Freezes the motion clock (`src/motion/clock.ts`, P4.1): `uTime` holds and the motion gate closes for every layer, so every stroke draws at rest. Shot and fidelity captures use it, and two `?noanim` shots taken apart in time are pixel-identical (`tests/e2e/motion.spec.ts`).
- `?calm=0..1`: the motion calm, for testing until the P5 settings panel sets it. Parsed like `?slow`, clamped to 0-1, and a missing or malformed value means 1 (full motion). Calm scales every motion's speed on the CPU (`uTime` advances by dt × calm, so a calm change never jumps a stroke) and its offsets and turns in the shader, once, in `src/motion/motion.glsl`. The wrap envelope that shrinks a looping stroke is not scaled. At 0 the time holds and the gate closes, as with `?noanim`.
- Motion (P4.1) is computed in the vertex shader from the shared uniforms `uTime` (seconds, wrapped at 3600), `uCalm`, `uFreeze` and `uMotionLayers`, plus each instance's layer (`iMeta.y`) and phase byte (`iMeta.z`). Every stroke material shares `motionClock`'s uniform objects. `motion.glsl` is spliced into `stroke.vert.glsl` at its `// #include motion.glsl` line by `spliceGlsl` in `material.ts`, and `src/motion/motion.ts` mirrors its gate and dispatch. `uMotionLayers` holds one bit per layer whose motion runs: `MOTION_LAYERS` (the sky, P4.2), and 0 while frozen or at calm 0, so a still stroke pays one AND, compare and branch. The app clock starts with every layer closed, and main.ts opens `MOTION_LAYER_MASK` only when a world boots, so the P1 canvas demo, `?strokesTest` and the bench, whose strokes share the sky layer, stay static. A layer's motion returns a `Motion` struct (centre offset, turn about the stroke normal, size gain), each 0 at rest. `motionOffset` multiplies the offset and turn by calm once. The gain is a loop's wrap envelope, so it runs at full depth whenever the gate is open and a stroke always shrinks to nothing as it jumps back, at every calm. With `?diag`, `__goghDiag().motion` is `{ time, calm, frozen, layers }`, where `layers` is the clock's open mask before frozen and calm close it.
- Sky swirl (P4.2, `src/motion/sky-swirl.ts`): every sky-layer stroke, the boundary haze included, moves along a field on the sphere of directions from the painter's eye: four Rankine-style vortices (the central double swirl, and eddies round the moon and the large lower-left star, authored under `vortices` in `assets/bake/starry-night/regions.json`, which the bake ignores) plus a slow drift about the vertical. Each stroke sweeps a short arc over an 8 s loop (3600 / 450), turns with the flow and shrinks to nothing at the wrap. Its displacement is at most `SKY_SWIRL_MAX_ANGLE` (0.034 rad) times its distance from the painter's eye, `SKY_SWIRL_REACH` (about 4 m) on the dome. At build the batch computes each sky stroke's exact reach, half a loop at its own speed (`skySwirlReach`), and each frame grows a chunk's cull sphere by its largest reach times calm while the sky's gate is open, and by nothing while it is closed, so under `?noanim`, at calm 0 and in the P1 canvas demo the cull spheres are the rest spheres. While the gate is open the haze's sky-light thin widens each stroke by its own reach, so a moved haze stroke never covers a disc.
- `?motiont=<s>`: testing only, honoured only in `--mode e2e` builds. Pins the motion time `uTime` at s seconds (wrapped at 3600) with the gate open, so a motion e2e is deterministic. The pin lives on the CPU in main.ts behind `import.meta.env.MODE === 'e2e'`, so a production build drops it and the shader reads nothing extra. `?noanim` still freezes and calm 0 still closes the gate. A negative or malformed value is ignored.
- `?motionphase=<f>`: testing only, honoured only in `--mode e2e` builds. Gives every stroke the motion phase f, wrapped to [0, 1), in place of its phase byte, so a pinned `?motiont` moves the whole sky coherently and the e2e can measure its displacement by block matching (`tests/e2e/motion.spec.ts`). main.ts calls `pinMotionPhase` before any material is built, and the materials carry it as the `MOTION_PHASE_PIN` define, so a production material never has it. e2e builds also install `window.__goghSkyShift(u, v, calm, time, phase)`, the TS mirror's predicted canvas shift of the dome stroke at canvas (u, v down) (`src/diag/swirl-probe.ts`), because Playwright cannot load `sky-swirl.ts` and its regions.json import. A negative or malformed value is ignored.
- `?shot`: boolean, honoured only together with `?diag`. Enables `window.__goghShot()` (canvas PNG data URL) and `preserveDrawingBuffer`. It takes no value.
- `?strokesTest`: boolean. Builds the deterministic stroke-engine test scene (`src/render/strokes/test-scene.ts`): 6000 strokes over 14 chunks, including a sky wall at z = -2 and a building wall behind the viewer at z = +6. It registers `strokes` in `__goghDiag()`, reporting `{ chunks, visibleChunks, total, visible, atlasMs }`.
- `?demo=canvas`: the P1 test canvas (`src/demo/canvas/`). It repaints the painting in about 200k strokes on a 4 × 3.17 m plane centred at (0, 1.6, -2.55), facing the XR origin. Like every scene without a world, the rig starts at the origin. Placement is deterministic for `?seed`, and no seed means seed 1. `&count=N` sets the stroke target instead of 200k, rounded and clamped to 10k-1M, so `&count=325000` draws the P0 default budget and `&count=500000` the max-beauty one. Placement lands within a few percent of the target (325000 gives 325,504). A non-numeric or malformed value means the default. It registers `canvas` in `__goghDiag()`: `{ seed, count, decodeMs, placeMs, batchMs, buildMs, strokes }`, where `strokes` holds the batch stats. Capture the P1-A2 shot with `bun scripts/capture-shot.ts "http://localhost:PORT/?diag&shot&demo=canvas&noanim&seed=1" docs/shots/p1-canvas.png --wait canvas`.

## Controls

`src/xr/input.ts` folds the XR gamepads (xr-standard mapping only) and the keyboard and mouse into one `Actions` frame per animation frame. `src/xr/input-keyboard.ts` captures the keys and mouse buttons. Locomotion (`src/locomotion/`) consumes `move`, `sprint`, `turn`, `teleportAim`, `teleportCommit`, `flightToggle` and `vertical`. Sticks use a 0.15 radial deadzone, and the frame carries `dt` clamped to 0.1 s.

| Action | XR | Desktop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `move` (x right, y forward, magnitude ≤ 1) | left stick | WASD |
| `sprint` (level) | none | Shift |
| `turn.x`, and `turn.snap` once per push past 0.7 with X dominant, re-armed once the whole stick is back inside the deadzone or Q/E is released | right stick X | Q (left), E (right) |
| `teleportAim`, then `teleportCommit` on release | right stick forward past 0.7 with Y dominant, held until below 0.3 | T held |
| `flightToggle` | right B | F |
| `vertical` (up positive) | right stick Y | Space (up), C (down) |
| `menu` | left button index 6 | M |
| `select` (level and edge) | either trigger | left mouse button, under pointer lock |
| `grip` (level and edge) | either grip | right mouse button, under pointer lock |
| `recenter`, once per 1 s hold | both stick clicks | R |

- xr-standard has no menu slot. Index 6 is the thumbrest in IWER's Quest profile, so `menu` reads only `pressed` there. Whether Quest exposes its left menu button to WebXR is unverified, and P5 revisits it.
- Left X (HUD), left Y (frame-rate step), right A (bench) and H (HUD) keep their own handlers and are not in the frame.
- Dominant axis: a right-stick snap needs |x| > |y| and an aim needs y > |x|, so a 45° push does neither.
- Desktop deviates from the plan's map: C moves down instead of Ctrl, because Ctrl+W closes the tab. While the canvas holds pointer lock, Space and the movement keys (WASD, Q, E, C) have their browser defaults cancelled. The Enter VR button blurs itself after a click, so Space cannot press it again. The click that requests pointer lock never selects, so desktop select and grip work only under lock.
- `input.suppressHeld()` is for mode switches. Every held button (controller buttons, F, M, R and the mouse buttons) and a held teleport aim (stick or T) report nothing until released once. The continuous axes are not suppressed, and neither are the keys that mirror them (WASD, Q/E, Space/C).
- The recenter hold starts on the second held frame, because the first frame's dt predates the press.
- With `?diag`, `__goghDiag().input` is `{ selectstart, actions }`, where `actions` is a copy of the last frame, or null before the first.

### Locomotion (P3.2 to P3.5)

- The camera sits in a rig, a three.js `Group` in the scene that carries the position and the rig yaw. XR's local-floor head pose and the desktop look stay inside it, so physical walking still works. `src/locomotion/step.ts` is the pure core and `src/locomotion/locomotion.ts` the wiring. XR and desktop share it. The bench never runs it, so its rig stays at the origin.
- Smooth movement: 2.0 m/s at full stick (`LocomotionSettings.moveSpeed`, clamped to 1.0-3.0) along the head yaw projected onto the ground, rig yaw included. Shift doubles it, capped at 3.0 m/s.
- Ground follow: the rig's y eases toward `WorldHandle.ground.heightAt` of the head's world XZ with an 80 ms time constant. With no world the floor is flat y = 0. While a world is loading the rig's y holds (not grounded) until its ground binds, so a spawn above y = 0 does not sink during the build. The desktop eye is 1.6 m above the rig.
- Seated mode adds its offset to the rig's y only, so physical head movement maps one to one into the world.
- Turning (`src/locomotion/turn.ts`, P3.3) rotates the rig yaw about the head's world XZ, not the rig origin, and re-solves the rig position so the head stays put. In XR the head is three's XR camera inside the rig, and on the desktop it is the eye, directly over the rig origin. Right is negative yaw. Snap mode (default) turns `snapAngleDeg` (30 or 45, default 30) once per `turn.snap`, at most once per 250 ms, and a request inside the cooldown is dropped. Smooth mode turns at `smoothTurnDegPerSec` (default 90, clamped to 30-180) times `turn.x`, and ignores `turn.snap`. The desktop Q and E keys feed the same `turn` action, so they snap too.
- The tunnel vignette (`src/ui/vignette.ts`, alpha in `src/ui/vignette-alpha.ts`, P3.3) is a black inside-out sphere of radius 1 m parented to the camera, with no depth test or write and `renderOrder` 999,000, below the perf HUD. It is a mesh, not post-processing, so fixed foveation stays intact. The fragment shader darkens by the angle off each eye's own forward axis: clear within 30°, ramping to full alpha at 50°. The ring is therefore centred on each eye's optical axis and carries no stereo disparity. On a real Quest the per-eye frusta are asymmetric, so it sits off the viewport centre there. `main.ts` compiles its program at boot with `renderer.compile`, so three links no new program on the first smooth move in XR. Whether the Quest driver still defers work to the first real draw is unverified.
- Its alpha is `LocomotionSettings.vignette` (default 0.6, clamped to 0-1) times the comfort factor: the larger of the smooth-move speed over the set `moveSpeed` and the smooth-turn rate over `smoothTurnDegPerSec`, capped at 1. Full stick, moving or smooth-turning, therefore reaches the full 0.6 strength at any speed setting, and half stick reaches 0.3. Snap turns add nothing. It eases with a 0.12 s time constant and lands on exactly 0 at rest, where the mesh is hidden and costs no draw call. It shows only while an XR session presents, so the desktop preview and its captures never draw it.
- Teleport (P3.4): `src/locomotion/teleport.ts` is the pure core. While `teleportAim` is held in walk mode, each frame traces a ballistic arc: 8 m/s along the aim ray, g = 9.8, 40 samples evenly spaced in time out to 15 m horizontally (or 3 s), with the landing bisected between the last sample above the ground and the first below it, then snapped onto `heightAt`. A landed arc is then resampled to 40 points evenly over its flight to the hit, so a short or steep-down arc still draws as a curve. In XR the aim ray is the right controller's target ray. `src/xr/right-controller.ts` creates both `renderer.xr.getController` slots at boot, parents them under the rig and picks the one whose input source has `handedness` `right`. On the desktop it is the camera's view ray from the eye. There is no aim while a world loads or while the right controller is untracked.
- A landing is rejected, in this order, where `boundaryFactor` is above 0 (from 45 m out, so nothing lands in the haze), where `WorldHandle.blocked(x, z)` is true, or where the ground normal is 35° or more from +Y. Its reason is then `boundary`, `footprint` or `slope`. An arc with no landing within 15 m has reason `none`. With no world the ground is flat y = 0, with no boundary and no footprints.
- On `teleportCommit` after a valid aim, the rig moves so the head's world XZ lands on the hit, and its y goes straight to the ground (plus the seated offset) with no 80 ms ease. An invalid aim does nothing. In XR the move happens under a blink: the vignette's `setFade` takes the view to black over 90 ms, the jump happens on that frame, and the view fades back over 90 ms. On the desktop the jump is instant. A spawn or `__goghPose` cancels a blink in progress and clears the last trace. An aim still held then traces again from the new pose on the next frame.
- While aiming, snap and smooth turns are ignored, Q and E included. Smooth movement still works. Teleport is gated at one point in `locomotion.ts`: walk mode with no exit drop running. Flight and the drop both switch off the aim and the commit.
- The arc (`src/locomotion/arc-mesh.ts`) is its own instanced mesh on the stroke material and brush atlas. It draws one ribbon stroke per arc segment, starting 0.25 m from the origin so it stays clear of the 0.1 m near plane, each turned to face the eye. It is warm yellow #f2c84b when valid and red-grey #8c706c otherwise, with a ring of 10 strokes of radius 0.35 m on the ground at a valid landing. The ring is translated toward the head along the hit-to-eye line by 10% of the eye distance, clamped to 0.3-0.6 m and at most a quarter of the distance, recomputed each frame (`ringPull`). It stays on the same eye ray through its centre but draws slightly larger and floats a little above the ground, with the arc ending just beyond its centre, so it stays on top of the ground strokes. A house or terrain crest nearer the eye than the pull still hides it. Hidden, it is `visible = false` and costs no draw. Shown, it is one draw per view, so two in XR without multiview. It is built on the first stroke batch's atlas, so in a stroke scene (`?painting`, `?strokesTest`, `?demo=canvas`) it shares the batch's program, and an aim before the batch loads draws no arc. The bare scene has no batch, so it builds its own 2048² atlas and the arc on the first aim. A boot that never aims pays nothing for them, and the first aim pays the atlas build, its upload and the program compile.
- Flight (P3.5): `src/locomotion/flight.ts` is the pure core, and it replaces `step.ts` for the frame while the rig flies. `flightToggle` (right B, desktop F) toggles it. The toggle takes effect from the next frame, and it is ignored while a world loads or a teleport blink runs. Entering flight hides any aim and turns ground follow off.
  - The left stick moves at `moveSpeed` (Shift sprints, capped at 3.0 m/s) along the head's full 3D forward, pitch included, so looking up and pushing forward climbs. Strafing stays level. `vertical` (right stick Y, desktop Space and C) adds 2.0 m/s straight up or down. Turning works as in walk mode.
  - Altitude is the head's height above the ground under the head's world XZ. Over the last 5 m below 40 m the upward speed scales by the share of that band left, and the rig's y is then hard-clamped so the altitude never exceeds 40 m. The clamp takes the head's height as at least its expected eye height (1.6 m, less the seated offset when seated), so a pose-less first XR frame (head y 0) cannot leave the first real pose above 40 m. The floor is on the rig: the ground plus the seated offset, where walk mode stands it, so a standing eye never sinks below 1.6 m over the terrain.
  - The boundary push reads the world's `boundaryFactor` at the head's XZ with y = 0, so only the horizontal radius from the origin counts. Starry Night's factor also rises with altitude, which the cap already handles. Above factor 0.8 an inward velocity grows linearly to 3 m/s at factor 1, so full stick stalls short of 50 m and an idle flight drifts back to factor 0.8. The vignette's comfort factor takes the larger of the stick speed and the push speed, so push drift shows it too. The head's horizontal radius is then hard-clamped at 55 m. Both apply only with a world boundary, so the bare scene has neither. Walk mode has no radius clamp and is unchanged.
  - The haze thickens with the same factor while flying: 0 at 0.8 rising to 1 at 1, eased with a 0.25 s time constant, landing on exactly 0 in walk mode. `WorldHandle.setHaze` sets `uHazeThickness` on every band material, and the vertex shader grows each haze stroke by up to 50% (`HAZE_GROWTH`). The haze is the sky layer's only strokes with `SKY_LIGHT_CULL_FLAG`, because the flags byte has no spare bit. A chunk holding haze always culls with its half-diagonal widened by the full growth, so a thickened stroke never reaches past its cull sphere.
  - Leaving flight starts the exit drop: the rig's y follows a smoothstep from its flight height to the ground (plus the seated offset) under the head, re-sampled every frame, and lands on it exactly 0.5 s later. Ground follow then resumes. Smooth movement works during the drop. Re-entering flight cancels it at the current height, and a spawn or `__goghPose` returns the rig to walk mode. A teleport aim held across a flight toggle (T or the stick) counts for nothing until it is released, so holding it through leaving flight neither draws the arc nor jumps.
- With `?diag` outside `?bench`, `__goghDiag().rig` is `{ position: [x, y, z], yaw, mode, altitude, haze, seated, grounded, speed, turnMode, vignette, aim, lastTeleport }`, where `mode` is `'walk'` or `'fly'`, `altitude` the head's height above the ground under it in metres, `haze` the haze thickening (0-1), and `vignette` the last frame's alpha. `aim` is the last frame's `{ active, valid, reason, hit }`, with `reason` one of `ok`, `slope`, `boundary`, `footprint` and `none`, and `hit` the landing [x, y, z] or null. `lastTeleport` is null until the first jump, then `{ from, to, t }`: `from` is the head's world XZ at the rig's ground y before the jump, `to` the landing, and `t` the `performance.now()` ms of the move, after the blink in XR. `yaw` is the rig's own yaw only, not the view's: on the desktop the mouse look does not change it, but Q and E do, and in XR it excludes the head yaw. `grounded` is false while a placed pose or a loading world holds the rig, in flight and during the exit drop, and `speed` is the stick speed in m/s, vertical flight included.
